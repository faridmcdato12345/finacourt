# Court Marketplace & Growth Platform — Project Skill

## 1. Purpose

This repository contains a Dockerized, web-first/PWA marketplace and SaaS platform for sports-court discovery, booking, payments, promotion, and court-owner growth.

The MVP foundation has already been implemented. Future development must **preserve and extend the existing stable architecture** rather than rebuild the application from scratch.

The platform serves four user groups:

1. **Players** — discover nearby courts, compare venues, view promotions and availability, and make reservations.
2. **Court owners/operators** — manage venues, courts, schedules, reservations, pricing, payments, promotions, customers, and growth performance.
3. **Sales partners/reps** — acquire and onboard court owners, register leads, assist setup, and earn commission based on approved attribution rules.
4. **Platform administrators** — manage marketplace quality, venues, users, payments, commissions, promotions, analytics, and platform configuration.

The strategic goal is no longer merely to provide court-booking software.

The platform must increasingly become a **customer-acquisition and demand-generation network for sports facilities**.

## 3. Product Positioning

The product should be positioned as:

> **A sports-court marketplace and growth platform that helps venues get discovered, convert player demand into bookings, fill unused court hours, retain customers, and measure the revenue generated by each acquisition channel.**

The platform must not compete primarily by having more court-management settings than competitors.

Court operations are supporting infrastructure.

The strategic differentiation is:

### 10.1 Conflict protection

Never rely only on frontend checks.

## 11. Payments

The application is web/PWA-first.

Payment flows should use a provider abstraction where practical.

Supported or future modes may include:

- Integrated online payment gateway
- Manual GCash/QR proof verification
- Pay at venue

Rules for integrated payments:

- Obtain valid booking state before creating payment attempt
- Store provider transaction/reference IDs
- Verify final payment through trusted server-side callback/webhook
- Never mark paid solely because a client opened a success URL
- Webhooks must be idempotent
- Payment state transitions must be auditable
- Refund/reversal behavior must be explicit

The platform monetization model may include a **customer service fee** on online bookings.

The venue's listed court price and the platform service fee must be modeled separately so reporting remains clear.

Example conceptual model:

```text
Court price           ₱500
Platform service fee   ₱25
Player total          ₱525
```

Do not hard-code fee percentages in booking logic. Use configurable pricing/fee rules.

# PART II — STRATEGIC DIFFERENTIATORS

## 13. Demand Intelligence — FIRST-CLASS PRIORITY

The platform must understand player demand **before a player chooses a venue**.

This is a strategic differentiator.

### 13.1 Track player intent

Track anonymous or account-linked marketplace events as appropriate, such as:

- search performed
- sport searched
- search location / area
- date requested
- time window requested
- price filters
- amenities filters
- result count
- zero-result search
- venue selected
- promotion selected
- booking started
- booking completed

Do not collect precise personal location longer than necessary unless required and properly consented to.

Prefer aggregated geographic demand whenever possible.

### 13.2 Demand aggregates

The system should eventually answer owner questions such as:

```text
Players searching within 5 km: 428

Badminton searches:   231
Pickleball searches:  147
Basketball searches:   50

Highest demand:
Friday 6 PM–10 PM

Unfulfilled searches:
86
```

Owners must never receive personally identifying information about arbitrary players simply because those players performed searches.

### 13.3 Unfulfilled demand

Zero-result or no-availability searches are valuable marketplace data.

Support concepts such as:

```text
Player searched:
Pickleball
Area X
Saturday 7–9 PM

No suitable availability
        ↓
Capture anonymous demand
        ↓
Optional "Notify me" intent
```

The platform should be able to aggregate this demand for owner/platform insights.

### 13.4 Demand heatmaps

Future owner analytics may aggregate demand by:

- sport
- city/area
- radius
- weekday
- hour/time range
- date
- price range

Do not overstate demand. Clearly distinguish:

- searches
- unique searching users
- booking-intent events
- completed bookings

## 14. Venue Promotion & Empty-Slot Recovery — FIRST-CLASS PRIORITY

Promotions are not merely voucher codes.

The platform should help owners distribute unsold inventory to relevant players.

### 14.1 Promotion targets

Allow promotion of:

- Venue
- Court
- Specific date/time slot
- Multiple selected slots
- Limited-time deal
- Open-play/event inventory where supported

### 14.2 Goal-based promotion UX

```text
What do you want to achieve?

○ Fill empty courts
○ Get new customers
○ Promote tonight
○ Increase weekday bookings
○ Bring previous customers back
○ Promote an event/open play
```

### 14.3 Empty-slot detection

Example:


Recommendations may include:

- promote slot
- reduce price
- create limited-time offer
- notify eligible marketplace users
- create owner share link

### 14.4 Marketplace promotion

The marketplace may surface promoted inventory in:

- search results
- deals pages
- available-tonight views
- nearby discovery
- owner share links
- notifications where permitted

Paid boosts may be introduced later, but normal useful promotions should not require a subscription merely to make the marketplace functional.

Dockerization is mandatory.

The normal project workflow must not require host-installed PHP, Composer, MySQL, Redis, or Node.js.

Only Git, Docker, and Docker Compose should be assumed on the development machine.

### 22.1 Docker services

Use the existing sound Docker setup.

Expected services may include:

- `app` — PHP/Laravel runtime
- `web` — Nginx/web server
- `db` — MySQL
- `node` — Vite/frontend tooling
- `redis` — only when required
- `queue` — when queued work exists
- `scheduler` — when scheduled work exists

Do not add infrastructure services without an actual requirement.

### 22.2 Environment rules

- Commit `.env.example`
- Never commit `.env` or secrets
- Use Compose service names for internal hosts
- Persist database data with named volumes
- Avoid hard-coded host paths
- Keep source/dependency mounts intentional
- Maintain Linux/WSL-compatible permissions

### 22.3 Commands

Document canonical commands such as:

```bash
docker compose up -d --build
docker compose exec app composer install
docker compose exec app php artisan migrate
docker compose exec app php artisan test
docker compose run --rm node npm install
docker compose run --rm node npm run build
```

Adapt to actual repository service names.

### 22.4 MySQL testing

Where locking/concurrency behavior matters, test against MySQL rather than relying solely on SQLite.

---

## 25. Database and Code Quality

- Use migrations for schema changes
- Add appropriate foreign keys and indexes
- Avoid N+1 queries
- Keep controllers thin
- Put complex booking/payment/promotion/attribution/commission logic into services/actions/domain classes as appropriate
- Avoid business logic in Vue components
- Use enums/value objects where they materially improve correctness
- Prefer explicit readable code
- Follow repository conventions

Before adding a new abstraction, inspect whether an equivalent already exists.

### 25.1 Analytics/event volume

Event tracking can become high volume.

Before adding large analytics tables:

- index for actual query patterns
- consider retention/aggregation strategy
- avoid loading raw event history unnecessarily
- avoid synchronous expensive aggregation during player requests

Use queued/background aggregation where justified.

   - files changed

## 37. Codex Decision Priorities

When several implementations are possible, prioritize in this order:

1. Correctness
2. Security/privacy
3. Tenant isolation
4. Data integrity
5. Backward compatibility with working MVP
6. Simplicity
7. Testability
8. User experience
9. Performance
10. Architectural elegance

Do not sacrifice correctness for shorter code.

---

## 38. Competitor-Aware Development Rule

Do not build a feature merely because another court platform has it.

Before implementing a large new capability, classify it as one of:

- **Table stakes** — needed for basic marketplace/booking usability
- **Strategic differentiator** — strengthens demand acquisition/growth loop
- **Customer-requested operational feature** — justified by real users
- **Speculative parity feature** — avoid unless explicitly approved

Strategic differentiators should receive priority over speculative parity features.

## 39. Definition of Next-Stage Success

The platform's next stage is successful when it can demonstrate this flow with real data:

```text
Player searches for a sport/time/location
        ↓
Platform records aggregated demand
        ↓
Relevant court has available/unsold inventory
        ↓
Owner receives useful demand insight
        ↓
Owner creates/approves promotion
        ↓
Player discovers promoted availability
        ↓
Player books and pays
        ↓
Booking source is attributed
        ↓
Owner sees new-customer/revenue impact
        ↓
Platform can re-engage customer later
```

The strategic KPI is not feature count.

The platform should increasingly prove:

> **We helped this venue get discovered, generate bookings, fill unused inventory, and retain customers.**
